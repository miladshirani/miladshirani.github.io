\section{Profile}

Scientific AI researcher and ML engineer with $10+$ years developing physics-based
simulation and machine learning methods for nonlinear solids and coupled
multiphysics systems. I train neural networks---including graph neural
networks---to solve coupled PDE systems by minimizing the total potential energy
or the FEM weak-form residual directly, with no labeled solution data and
Dirichlet conditions imposed exactly. The networks share their discretization
(shape functions, quadrature, boundary conditions) with the finite-element solver,
so every result is compared node by node with a converged Newton--Raphson reference.
I also build differentiable simulators: a matrix-free Newton--Krylov finite-element
solver and autodiff-based meshless peridynamics for fracture and delamination.
A neural operator I trained predicted unseen cases $\sim 35$--$250\times$ faster per
query than FEM on a benchmark problem, and across four coupled systems my networks
match converged finite-element solutions to within $0.05$--$3\%$.
Open-source ML development and startup experience translating
scientific ML methods into engineering models.

\section{Research \& Professional Experience}

\jobheader{Postdoctoral Associate --- Yale University}{Sept 2023 -- Present}
\subline{Department of Biomedical Engineering, New Haven, CT}

\begin{itemize}

% ── Differentiable simulation ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Differentiable scientific computing:}
Developed a differentiable, autodiff-based meshless peridynamics solver for
nonlinear solid mechanics, modeling brittle fracture, pressure-driven (hydraulic)
fracture, and delamination (Mode I/mixed-mode peel) without remeshing; achieved an
$\mathbf{8-10\times}$ \textbf{runtime speedup} and $\mathbf{9.7\times10^{-11}}$
\textbf{agreement} with a validated reference solution. Engineered a matrix-free
dynamic-relaxation scheme to overcome Newton--Raphson tangent singularities and
converge through unstable crack propagation. In collaboration with
Prof.~Ali Javili (Bilkent University);
\href{https://miladshirani.github.io/research-peridynamic-fracture.html}{project page}.

% ── Differentiable FEA ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Differentiable FEA (matrix-free Newton--Krylov):}
Built an \href{https://github.com/miladshirani/Differentiable-FEA}{open-source}
PyTorch finite-element solver for large-strain hyperelasticity that never assembles
the stiffness matrix: residuals and tangents come from automatic differentiation, and
each Newton step is solved by preconditioned Krylov (conjugate-gradient) iteration
using only Jacobian--vector products. Exact design gradients follow from the implicit
function theorem with one adjoint solve. Matches FEniCSx to $<10^{-13}$ (relative);
an element-wise operator variant makes the GPU tangent product
$\mathbf{11.6\times}$ \textbf{faster} (40{,}000 Q4 elements, T4), and a complete
10{,}000-element solve takes $\mathbf{2.9}$~s
(\href{https://miladshirani.github.io/research-differentiable-fea.html}{project page}).

% ── Operator learning + energy-minimizing networks ────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Label-free physics-informed networks and neural operators for nonlinear solids:}
Designed variational, energy-minimizing PINNs and DeepONets in PyTorch and JAX
for liquid crystal elastomers (coupled finite-strain deformation and director
fields), trained on the total potential energy over FEM/NEM discretizations
(Delaunay/Voronoi, perforated domains) with Gauss quadrature and exactly
enforced boundary conditions. A single operator trained over a load range predicts
the full deformation and director fields in $\sim 0.5$--$0.7$~ms, \textbf{$\mathbf{\sim 35}$--$\mathbf{250\times}$
faster per query} than a warm FEM solve (156-node plane-strain benchmark, CPU;
training repaid after $\sim 100$--$800$ queries), with $\sim 0.7\%$ (shear) and
$1.6$--$3\%$ (uniaxial) displacement error on unseen stretches against the same-mesh
FEM reference. Derived quasiconvexity, rank-one convexity, and Legendre--Hadamard
inequalities from the energy loss and used them as validation criteria:
all stored trajectories are strongly elliptic strict local minimizers of the
discretized energy
(\href{https://miladshirani.github.io/research-physics-enforced-operator-learning.html}{project page}).

% ── Physics-informed GNNs ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Data-free, discretization-consistent physics-constrained GNNs:}
Trained graph neural networks to solve coupled multiphysics PDE systems as
implicit time-steppers by minimizing the FEM weak-form residual directly, enforcing
conservation laws and thermodynamic consistency with no labeled solution data.
The graph is the mesh (or its Voronoi dual) and the network shares shape
functions, quadrature and boundary conditions with the FEM solver, so each network
is geometry-informed, and the weak-form residual serves as a built-in error
monitor. Retrained per case. Demonstrated on two systems against reference
Newton--Raphson FEM solutions, in both JAX and PyTorch (residuals parity-checked
to $\sim 10^{-12}$):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,
itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt, parsep=0pt,
label=\textcolor{midgray}{\textendash}]

\item \textbf{PI-GCN for chemoelasticity (gel swelling):}
Graph-convolutional network with residual connections for a transient, finite-strain,
coupled 4-field system (displacement, pressure, chemical potential, concentration);
block-weighted weak-form loss and Adam\,$\to$\,L-BFGS training, FEM and meshless NEM
back ends, plate with a hole. Final-state error $0.24\%$ in displacement and $0.73\%$
in concentration over 50 implicit steps (441 nodes).

\item \textbf{PI-EPD-GNN for thermoelasticity:}
MeshGraphNets-style Encode--Process--Decode network for transient heat conduction
coupled to finite-deformation elasticity (u--p--$\theta$), with Taylor--Hood
elements, a Voronoi message-passing graph, and a loss preconditioned by the
rest-state tangent. Errors of $0.14\%$ (displacement) and $0.011\%$ (temperature)
on a plate with a hole (PyTorch).

\end{itemize}

% ── Inverse constitutive modeling ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Inverse constitutive modeling / data-driven material discovery:}
Developed PINN-based inverse frameworks in JAX and PyTorch for learning
polyconvex stored-energy functions for isotropic and anisotropic materials,
including fibrous composites and biological tissues, directly from FEA-generated
datasets, with polyconvexity enforced as a hard architectural constraint.

% ── PI-FNO ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{PI-FNO --- open-source neural operator deployment:}
Designed, implemented, and open-sourced
\href{https://github.com/miladshirani/PI-FNO}{PI-FNO},
a Physics-Informed Fourier Neural Operator for 5-class valvular heart disease
classification from phonocardiograms. Enforced physiological constraints
(cardiac periodicity and S1/S2 frequency hierarchy) as hard architectural priors,
with a learnable frequency mask initialized from cardiac acoustics and adapted
end-to-end; achieved \textbf{97.5\% test accuracy} across 1,000 recordings.

% ── Multiphysics infrastructure ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\item \textbf{Multiphysics simulation infrastructure:}
Built high-fidelity simulation frameworks in FEniCSx, COMSOL, JAX and PyTorch for
fibrous biomaterials, hydrogel and elastomer swelling, thermoelasticity, and
diffusion--mechanics coupling, with Newton--Raphson solvers using autodiff
tangents. Implemented large-deformation swelling across multiple material
models and compared penalty, Lagrange-multiplier, and augmented-Lagrangian
constraint formulations. Currently extending the hydrogel swelling model to
biological tissues and to delamination of layered hydrogel systems. Simulation
datasets serve as ground truth for surrogate training and benchmarking.

\end{itemize}
