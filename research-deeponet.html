Soft Matter · Physical AI

DeepONet for
nematic elastomers.

An operator-learning network that takes the load level as input and returns the full deformation and director fields, so one trained model covers a whole load ramp.

Nematic elastomers are rubber-like polymer networks coupled to an orientational order, so the deformation \( \boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{\chi}(\boldsymbol{X}) \) and the unit director \( \boldsymbol{d}(\boldsymbol{X}) \) must be found together. The full theory, the constitutive model and the stability conditions are on the Physics-Enforced Neural Networks page. The stored energy depends on both fields and on the director gradient,

\[ W=W(\boldsymbol{F},\boldsymbol{d},\nabla\boldsymbol{d}),\qquad \boldsymbol{F}=\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi},\qquad \boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}=1, \]

and equilibrium is a stationary point of the total potential energy. This is the problem the networks learn to solve, as in the paper below[1].

Operator learning

A DeepONet learns an operator, not a single solution. In the standard DeepONet construction, a branch network encodes the input, here the load level, and a trunk network encodes the position in the body, and their combination gives the fields at that position. After training, any load on the ramp is evaluated with one forward pass.

Simulations

The same thin strip as on the PENN page is solved under four loading cases: shear and uniaxial stretch, each with and without a circular hole. Each animation shows the load ramp, with the network prediction, the finite-element reference and the pointwise error side by side: displacement magnitude on top, director angle below.

Animations are interpolated between the stored load steps for smooth playback. The reference in every panel is a finite-element solution of the same discretized problem. Work in progress.

What is checked

The computed solutions are checked against the necessary stability conditions described on the PENN page: strong ellipticity at every Gauss point, and the second variation of the discretized energy at every load step. All stored trajectories pass both checks. A passing check means the states are consistent with these necessary conditions. It does not mean the network matches the finite-element solution; that is what the animations show, and the network remains visibly less accurate than FEA, especially in director angle.

Reference

[1] M. Shirani, P. H. Gueldner, M. Khidoyatov, J. L. Warren, F. Ninno, Physics-Consistent Neural Networks for Learning Deformation and Director Fields in Microstructured Media with Loss-Based Validation Criteria, arXiv:2603.06939 (2026). arxiv.org/abs/2603.06939 ↗
