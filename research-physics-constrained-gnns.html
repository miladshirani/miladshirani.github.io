Physics AI · Coupled Multiphysics

Data-free
physics-constrained GNNs.

Graph neural networks that solve coupled multiphysics problems as implicit time-steppers by minimizing the finite-element weak-form residual directly. There are no labeled solution fields: the network is not trained on simulation data, and the physics itself provides the training signal. Every result is compared node by node with a converged Newton–Raphson solution.

At a glance

Problem
Transient, finite-strain coupled PDE systems, here gel swelling and thermoelasticity, solved with no labeled solution data and Dirichlet conditions imposed exactly.
Method
Graph neural networks trained by minimizing the FEM weak-form residual directly, as implicit time-steppers. The mesh itself becomes the graph; JAX and PyTorch.
Physics & math
Shape functions, quadrature and boundary conditions are shared with the FEM discretization. Conservation laws and thermodynamic consistency enter through the weak form, and the residual is an intrinsic, physics-based error measure.
Result
PI-GCN: 0.24% displacement and 0.73% concentration error. PI-EPD-GNN: 0.14% displacement and 0.011% temperature error. Both against Newton–Raphson FEM; residuals parity-checked to about 10−12.

Idea

Coupled multiphysics problems rarely come with labeled solution data, but they always come with a weak form. A conventional data-driven surrogate is fitted to simulation output. Here there is none: the network is trained to make the finite-element weak-form residual small at each implicit time step, so the physics itself provides the training signal. Conservation laws and thermodynamic consistency are enforced through that residual rather than learned from examples.

The mesh itself becomes the graph, or its Voronoi dual. Because the network shares its discretization (shape functions, quadrature, boundary conditions) with the finite-element solver, every network is geometry-informed, and the weak-form residual provides an intrinsic, physics-based error measure. Each network is retrained per case.

PI-GCN for chemo-elasticity. A transient, finite-strain, coupled four-field problem on a plate with a hole (441 nodes, 50 implicit steps). The loss is a block-weighted weak-form residual, trained with Adam followed by L-BFGS, with both finite-element and meshless natural-element (NEM) back ends. The errors above are final-state errors over the 50 steps.

PI-EPD-GNN for thermoelasticity. Transient heat conduction coupled to finite-deformation elasticity (displacement, pressure, temperature) on a plate with a hole, in PyTorch. The network uses Taylor–Hood elements and a Voronoi message-passing graph, and its loss is preconditioned by the rest-state tangent.

What this does and does not show

  • Two systems are demonstrated, each on a plate with a hole, against a reference on the same discretization.
  • Each network is retrained per case. No generalization across geometries or parameters is claimed here, and no speed comparison with FEM is made.
  • The comparison is with FEM on the same mesh, so it measures how well the network solves the discretized problem, not the discretization error itself.
